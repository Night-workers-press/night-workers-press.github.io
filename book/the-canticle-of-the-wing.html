The bookLe livre

The last poems, selected — and the strangest late turn in Rostand's work. The title piece is a hymn to powered flight written while the first aeroplanes were still killing their pilots: Icarus rewritten as a man who succeeded. Around it stand the war poems, the occasional verse, the elegies. Read after Cyrano, they show a poet who had spent his life defending the beautiful useless gesture watching the century invent a useful one, and refusing to be disappointed.

Les derniers poèmes, choisis — et le virage le plus étrange de l'œuvre de Rostand. La pièce-titre est un hymne au vol motorisé écrit alors que les premiers avions tuaient encore leurs pilotes : Icare réécrit en homme qui réussit. Autour, les poèmes de guerre, les vers de circonstance, les élégies. Lus après Cyrano, ils montrent un poète qui avait passé sa vie à défendre le beau geste inutile regardant le siècle en inventer un utile, et refusant d'en être déçu.

THE POEMS

I THE CANTICLE OF THE WING

So it is at the moment when men were calling the Century

drunk on nothing, and the soul

out of employment, that we see Prowess resurrected all at once

and the Exploit born again!

The Hero, who had withdrawn under his tent

like the Greek hero,

has just torn away the trembling canvas

in order to fly off with it!

There were a few wires, this canvas, and the void...

And man flew.

We did not read it in the fables of Ovid:

we saw it.

In vain the Icarian spectre clings

to the slender fuselage.

It is time to sing the Canticle of the Wing:

man is afraid of nothing.

Nothing is impossible any longer to the man who plots

his eternal conspiracy,

since he has just sat down on the invisible spine

of an invisible wave!

Wing, tear the wheel out of the greasy kiss of the grass,

and climb into the summer sky

in the glory of the risk and the superb disgust

with being safe!

Tremble in the river wind! dance in the eddy off the woods!

and lean over a moment,

so that the fields of home may gild the earthly Bird

with a reflection of wheat!

And you, our Sun, the most beautiful thing a man could wish

to be looking for as he dies,

receive the new Eagle our Lark is making of herself

by growing beyond measure!

When they saw that man had, in the mystery,

built the Wing, the Heavens

knew they were about to see which people on this earth

was the daring one.

France, we knew perfectly well that in every History

the men of your soil

would always be standing on every promontory

from which a flight can be taken;

but that they should have taken it so high, at a moment when flute players

were already leading your funeral,

is something worth stopping over for one minute

in order to feel proud!

Bright country who never occupied yourself

with unreal things in vain,

others, more than you, were able to sigh: "Wings!"

at a time when the Wing did not exist;

but the moment the Wing existed, the moment it seemed possible

that man, indignant at walking,

should make himself, with the sky for a target,

both the arrow and the archer;

the moment the Brave, invited to fly by the cry of the two brothers,

full of dread,

felt that first of all there had to be the Reckless,

and that they must be skilful,

and must be light, and, with the flame in their eyes,

must be in the habit,

whenever an idea or a pair of wings has to be tried,

of dying first;

the moment one had to die for what had only just been born,

to fall so that others might fly,

France felt the shiver by which she recognises

that her destiny lies there!

It was enough for her to know that a wing had been found,

of canvas and of reeds,

for her to be nothing but one immense brood

of impatient birds!

For the virtue of a heart all of whose wounds

open toward the East

is not to wait until the roads are safe,

and to be impatient!

Ever since this imperious thing existed

that insists we go to the skies,

France has been the country of mothers with sad eyes

but glorious foreheads!

Ah, how they went off in high spirits,

our sons, young and mad!

For one dies for the blue as one dies for Greece

when one is of our country!

At the moment when they are about to take, in a noise of squall,

the road that has no road,

they say goodbye to us with a nod of the helmet,

and then they raise a hand!

Into the saddened sky of our landscape

they have flown away.

They have obliged us to lift our faces.

They have consoled us.

They are great heroes, they are pure athletes,

these crossers of our seas,

the ones whose heads the wind itself has crowned

with the blue laurel of the air!

Ah, those who leave everything so as to see the summits

only when they are leaning over —

of those one may say that they are truly sublime

and truly detached!

When their golden Victory passes over the countryside,

everything is prodigious!

Fires have to be lit to guide them

from mountain to mountain!

The small peasant, grown a cubit taller,

shouts dialect at the sky;

kings have the eyes of Magi out of Chaldea;

the people are up on the roofs!

To these rousers of divine eagerness,

praise for ever and ever!

A song in the valley! An ode on the hills!

A hymn on the summits!

Ever since men have flown far from our poor lands,

those who do not fly

open you oftener, rudimentary wings

that are nothing but arms!

We are taking back hope, and prides, and our courage,

ever since we came to love

those who mix their shadow with the shadows of the clouds

on the slope of the mountains!

In vain the charlatans chalk their patter on the asphalt

or on the paving stones:

it no longer halts around their sales talk,

the crowd with the raised eyes!

The crowd with the raised eyes, singing, hurrying its steps

down toward the plain,

no longer sees the dealers in irony or in hatred

whom lowered eyes admire!

The Wing is victorious. It passes, passes again,

and insists on passing again,

knowing that on the ground its calm shadow wipes out

what has to be wiped out!

When they have disappeared into the celestial dust,

these Champions we were speaking of,

look at the sand, then, and see what is left

of your divisions!

Everything is wiped out! and the sky Lamartine foretold

sees, as Hugo foresaw,

the bird that was cut out of a lateen sail

flying off into its indigo!

It is time to sing the Canticle of the Wing,

which insists that we should have

a road for ever climbing, and parallel

to the path of the sunbeams!

In this country where the soul is always rumbling,

the country Liberty comes out of,

the young Echo must answer the old Song of Departure

with a Song of the Climb!

Bonaparte, they are — even if, deep in the poems,

your Eagle should be angry at it —

the sons of your soldiers who will themselves fly

from steeple to steeple!

Dear Conquerors, waited for at the opened window

of the highest tower!

When one gets into a country by way of the sky,

one brings it love!

Ah, the first time we saw man separate himself

from the mire,

we understood perfectly well that a sort of Archangel

was going to be got ready!

A chivalry is opening a cavalcade

that goes on surpassing everything,

and whose happiness it is to be stained as yet

with nothing but its own blood!

How beautiful they are, on the Mountain, the Wings

of those who have come

to bring us the Good News, and news

of unknown battles!

Battles of space! unspeakable conquests!

Triumphs without remorse!

Glory to all those by whom these things were done!

Rostand in English

Rostand is known in English for one play. Cyrano de Bergerac has never been out of print, never off the stage and never short of translators; everything else he wrote — six more plays, two books of poems, a great deal of occasional verse — is almost entirely missing. What English versions exist are mostly Edwardian, made within a few years of the originals by different hands, and they read now like translations of a translation: a fog of thees and thous laid over a writer who was, in French, fast, funny, theatrical and modern. Rostand in English is the first attempt to change that properly — one translator, one series, one voice.

Rostand n'est connu en anglais que pour une pièce. Cyrano de Bergerac n'a jamais cessé d'être imprimé, joué, retraduit ; tout le reste — six autres pièces, deux recueils de poèmes, quantité de vers de circonstance — manque presque entièrement. Les versions anglaises existantes sont pour l'essentiel édouardiennes, faites à quelques années des originaux par des mains différentes, et se lisent aujourd'hui comme des traductions de traduction : un brouillard d'archaïsmes posé sur un écrivain qui était, en français, rapide, drôle, théâtral et moderne. Rostand in English est la première tentative d'y remédier vraiment — un traducteur, une collection, une voix.
